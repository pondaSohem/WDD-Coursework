SDG 4: Target 4.3

Equal Access to Affordable Technical, Vocational and Tertiary Education

What is Target 4.3?

Target 4.3 specifically calls for ensuring equal access for all women and men to affordable and quality technical, vocational and tertiary education, including university. Unlike primary education which focuses on basics, Target 4.3 addresses post-secondary pathways that lead to meaningful careers.

  • Focuses on higher education and vocational training
  • Emphasizes affordability as a key barrier
  • Requires equal access regardless of gender, income, or location
  • Recognizes diverse pathways beyond traditional university

Why Affordability Matters

High tuition fees exclude millions from higher education. In many countries, the cost of a university degree equals several years of average income.

  • Student debt crisis affects economic mobility
  • Low-income families often cannot afford tuition, books, and living expenses
  • Affordable education breaks the cycle of poverty
  • Free or low-cost tertiary education increases social mobility

Types of Post-Secondary Education

Target 4.3 covers diverse educational pathways beyond secondary school:

  • Technical Education (engineering, IT, healthcare technology)
  • Vocational Training (construction, automotive, culinary arts)
  • Community College and Associate Degrees
  • University Bachelor's, Master's, and Doctoral Programs
  • Professional Certifications and Trade Schools

Barriers to Access

Even when education is available, many cannot access it due to:

  • High tuition and mandatory fees
  • Lack of institutions in rural or remote areas
  • Indirect costs: transportation, housing, materials
  • Digital divide limiting online learning access
  • Insufficient scholarship and grant programs
  • Family income constraints requiring students to work instead of study

The Way Forward for 2030

Achieving Target 4.3 requires coordinated action from governments, educational institutions, and international organizations. Key strategies include tuition-free public higher education, expanded scholarship programs, income-based repayment systems, and investment in open educational resources.

The Affordability Crisis in Higher Education

Globally, the cost of post-secondary education has risen dramatically faster than inflation and household income. This affordability crisis directly undermines Target 4.3, which calls for equal access regardless of economic status.

Hidden Costs Beyond Tuition

Even when tuition is subsidized, students face numerous additional expenses that create barriers:

  • Textbooks and learning materials - Can cost hundreds or thousands per year, with prices rising faster than inflation.
  • Housing and living expenses - Many students cannot afford to relocate to where education is offered.
  • Transportation costs - Long commutes or lack of public transit limits access.
  • Technology requirements - Laptops, software, and reliable internet are increasingly essential.
  • Lost wages - Time spent studying means forgone income, a barrier for families depending on young workers.
"The single biggest predictor of whether a student will complete a post-secondary credential is their family's income. Target 4.3 cannot be achieved without addressing systemic economic barriers."

Regional Variations in Affordability

Affordability challenges vary dramatically by region. In countries with free or low-cost public universities (Germany, Norway, Brazil, Argentina), barriers shift to living costs and competitive admission. In nations with high tuition (United States, United Kingdom, Japan), student debt becomes a lifelong burden. Low-income countries face a different challenge: even minimal fees exceed what most families can pay.

Global Solutions Advancing Target 4.3

Successful Policy Models

  • Tuition-Free Public Higher Education - Germany eliminated undergraduate tuition fees for all students, including international students, resulting in increased enrollment and diversity. Argentina's public universities have been tuition-free since 1949.
  • Income-Contingent Loan Systems - Australia and England use systems where graduates repay loans as a percentage of income, protecting those who earn less after graduation.
  • Open Educational Resources (OER) - British Columbia's OER initiative has saved students over $30 million in textbook costs by promoting free, openly licensed materials.
  • Community-Based Vocational Hubs - Kenya's "Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) Authority" has established affordable training centers in rural areas, reaching previously excluded populations.
  • Need-Based Scholarship Guarantees - The United States' "Pell Grant" program provides funding based on financial need, while many universities now offer "no-loan" financial aid for low-income students.

Technology's Role in Reducing Costs

Digital transformation offers promising pathways to affordability. Massive Open Online Courses (MOOCs) from platforms like Coursera, edX, and Khan Academy provide free or low-cost education from top universities. Micro-credentials and digital badges offer affordable, targeted skill development without full-degree costs. Open universities (e.g., Open University UK, Indira Gandhi National Open University) use distance learning to reduce infrastructure and delivery costs while expanding access.

Investment in affordable post-secondary education generates returns far exceeding costs. Every dollar spent on need-based financial aid yields approximately $6 in increased lifetime earnings and tax revenue.

Success Stories in Affordable Education

Germany's Tuition-Free Transformation

When Germany eliminated tuition fees for all public universities in 2014, enrollment surged by over 30% among low-income students. International enrollment increased 50%, bringing diverse perspectives without creating debt burdens. Graduates enter the workforce without the stress of loan repayment, enabling entrepreneurship and public service careers.

Brazil's PROUNI Scholarship Program

Brazil's University for All Program (PROUNI) provides full and partial scholarships to low-income students at private universities, with quotas for racial minorities and students with disabilities. Since 2005, over 2 million students have accessed higher education who otherwise could not afford it.

Rwanda's TVET Expansion

Rwanda's Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) system has built 39 model training centers across the country, with sliding-scale fees based on family income. Enrollment increased 400% between 2015 and 2023, with 60% of graduates finding employment within six months.

University of the People

This US-accredited, tuition-free online university serves students from over 200 countries. Students pay only assessment fees (as low as $60 per exam), and those who cannot afford fees receive scholarships. Over 50,000 students have enrolled, many the first in their families to pursue higher education.

Take Action: Supporting Affordable Education for All

Individuals, communities, and institutions can advance Target 4.3 through concrete actions that reduce cost barriers and expand access to post-secondary education.

What Students Can Do

  • Advocate for open educational resources - Petition your institution to adopt free or low-cost textbooks and materials.
  • Support peer mentorship programs - Help first-generation and low-income students navigate financial aid and academic systems.
  • Share scholarship information - Many scholarships go unused due to lack of awareness; spread the word in your community.
  • Volunteer with college access programs - Organizations like "College Possible" and "Strive for College" need tutors and mentors.

What Institutions and Policymakers Can Do

  • Eliminate or reduce tuition fees - Redirect funding to make public higher education free or income-based.
  • Expand need-based financial aid - Ensure scholarships reach those with greatest economic need.
  • Invest in open digital infrastructure - Create and share open educational resources, reducing textbook costs.
  • Establish community-based learning centers - Bring vocational and technical training to rural and underserved areas.
"Target 4.3 is not just about building more universities. It's about ensuring that when a young person has the talent and drive to pursue post-secondary education, their family's bank account does not determine their future."
